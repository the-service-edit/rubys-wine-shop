Your call 1

How the box on your forms is set

Either way, everyone can see it and change it before they submit.

Unticked (my recommendation). People tick it to join. Fewer sign-ups at first, but every one of them chose you, so fewer unsubscribes and spam reports. Ticked, they untick if they don't want it. More sign-ups. Australia's regulator, the ACMA, advises against pre-ticked boxes, so this is your decision and I'll set it up as you choose.
Your call 2

Who in Square gets that first email

My recommendation: three or more separate visits in the past 12 months, with the most recent in the last 6 months.

There's no legal number. The Spam Act lets you email people you have a genuine, ongoing relationship with, and the ACMA says a one-off purchase isn't enough. So we choose a line you can stand behind:

One visit is a one-off purchase, which the ACMA rules out. These customers are asked at the counter and through a link on your receipts instead. Three or more is a pattern. It's someone who chooses Ruby's. A visit in the last 6 months keeps it current. Someone who came three times in 2024 and never again isn't an ongoing relationship.

Two rules that come with it:

We only email them about what they already buy from you: wine, the bar and events at Ruby's. That relevance is part of what makes it hold up. Anyone who unsubscribes is removed straight away and never added back.

Square's customer directory shows each customer's visits and last visit, so the list takes minutes to pull. I'm not a lawyer. This follows the ACMA's published guidance; check with your own adviser if you'd like certainty.
